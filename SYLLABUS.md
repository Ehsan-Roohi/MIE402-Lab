## Course information

This course integrates lectures, pre-lab preparation, hands-on experiments, data analysis, and technical reporting. The Fall 2026 offering covers Laboratories 1 through 6 only.

## References and software

No required textbook.

Figliola & Beasley, Theory and Design for Mechanical Measurements, 6th ed., Wiley, 2015.

Beckwith et al., Mechanical Measurements, 6th ed., Pearson, 2006.

Wheeler & Ganji, Introduction to Engineering Experimentation, 3rd ed., Pearson, 2009.

MATLAB and Simulink are required for selected pre-labs, analysis, and reports.

## Course policies

Each pre-lab assignment is due on Canvas before the student's own laboratory section begins. Solutions may be posted after the final section for that experiment; late pre-labs will not be accepted.

Reports are due on Canvas at 11:59 PM on the dates listed in the tentative course schedule. A report grade will be reduced by 25% for each day or partial day it is late.

Follow the laboratory report template and the stated page limit for each submission.

Do not use screenshots of the MATLAB (Python) figure window in pre-labs or reports. Export figures in a high-resolution format such as PNG, EPS, or PDF. Group related data in a single figure when comparison improves the analysis.

Students may discuss approaches to pre-labs, but copying solutions or sharing MATLAB (Python) or Simulink source files is prohibited. All submitted work must comply with the UMass Amherst Academic Integrity Policy.

Make-up laboratories are possible only when an instructor/GTA and appropriate space and equipment are available. Because equipment changes between experiments, a make-up may not be possible after the course moves to the next laboratory topic.

Notify the instructor during the first two weeks about university-approved schedule conflicts. Disability-related accommodation requests should be initiated with Disability Services as early as possible; accommodations are not retroactive.

Any pre-lab or report may include a brief oral verification. An inability to explain submitted work may result in loss of credit for that submission.

Ask questions of general interest during lecture and individual questions during office hours. Use email when neither option is practical. Clearly show work in every submission to receive partial credit.

## Laboratory sequence

| Lab | Experiment | Primary focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Dynamic data sampling and frequency analysis | Sampling, aliasing, and spectral interpretation |
| 2 | Single pendulum | Dynamic response and model-data comparison |
| 3 | Double pendulum | Coupled motion and nonlinear behavior |
| 4 | 1-DOF system - free response | Natural frequency and damping |
| 5 | 1-DOF system - forced response | Frequency response and resonance |
| 6 | Radiometer | Radiometer movement measurement |
