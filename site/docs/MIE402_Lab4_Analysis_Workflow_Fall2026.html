Lab 4 — Free-response analysis workflow

This is a supplementary explanation of the existing experiment, not a change in required hardware or seven-case procedure. Canvas and the released laboratory instructions remain authoritative for section-specific directions.

1. What to bring and record

Measure the total moving mass (cart plus all mounted and moving attachments), identify left and right calibrated springs, count actual brake magnets, record sample rate, release displacement and original CSV filename. A brake-assembly count is not interchangeable with a magnet count.

2. Calibrate each spring vertically

For hanging mass mh [kg], static extension Δ0 [m], spring stiffness k [N/m], and gravity g = 9.81 m/s², equilibrium requires kΔ0 = mhg. For displacement q [m] from that equilibrium, mhq̈ = mhg − k(Δ0+q) = −kq. Gravity cancels, leaving mhq̈+kq=0. If Graphical Analysis fits force as F(t) = a sin(bt+cfit)+d, its b is angular frequency [rad/s], not frequency in Hz, and cfit [rad] is a phase—not the damping coefficient. For light damping and negligible spring mass, k ≈ mhb²; otherwise investigate damping and effective mass.

3. Perform seven comparable cart runs

Run(s)ConfigurationHypothesis to test
1Base: original springs/mass, no added brake magnetsMeasure residual damping; it is not zero
2–3Add one mass, then a second; maintain same springsNatural frequency decreases as mass increases
4–5Two magnets, then four; maintain spring pairDecay becomes faster; weigh all hardware
6–7Return to base mass; use two alternate spring combinationsNatural frequency follows equivalent stiffness

Level the track; ensure both springs stay in pre-tension. In Vernier Graphical Analysis select the correct cart serial number, check the wheel encoder is rolling without slip, zero the position near equilibrium, start Collect and release without a push. Follow the supplied local sampling constraint (at most 25 Hz), collect multiple cycles and measurable decay, and export time and position to a distinct unmodified CSV for each run.

4. Derive the analysis quantities

Displacement x(t) [m] is relative to horizontal equilibrium. Both preloaded springs oppose a small displacement: ΔF1 = −k1x and ΔF2 = −k2x. Therefore keq = k1 + k2 [N/m]. Assuming a viscous-equivalent brake force −cẋ, with c [N·s/m], Newton's law yields mẍ+cẋ+keqx=0.

After dividing by m, define ωn = √(keq/m) [rad/s] and ζ = c/(2mωn) [dimensionless]. Roots of the characteristic equation are −ζωn ± iωd in the underdamped case, where ωd = ωn√(1−ζ²) [rad/s]. Their real part gives α = ζωn = c/(2m) [s⁻¹]; the imaginary part gives oscillation. The response is x(t)=e−αt[C cos(ωdt)+D sin(ωdt)].

5. Turn one CSV into measurements

  1. Preserve raw CSV; select the free-response interval after hands have released the cart. Confirm position units (m, cm, or mm) and timestamps; do not apply a linear detrend blindly.
  2. Subtract estimated equilibrium offset, and inspect for actual drift. Use reliable same-sign peaks above the noise floor.
  3. Estimate damped period Td = (tlast−tfirst)/N [s], then ωd = 2π/Td [rad/s].
  4. Fit ln(p/(1 m)) against peak time [s]. The slope is −α [1/s]. Alternatively use δ = ln(pi/pi+N)/N = αTd.
  5. Compute ωn = √(ωd² + α²), ζ = α/ωn, and c = 2mα [N·s/m]. Compare predicted undamped frequency √(keq/m)/(2π) [Hz].
  6. Inspect time series, log-peak fit, full-response fit and its residuals, plus FFT magnitude versus frequency. The FFT peak near fd is a check, not a standalone damping estimate.

6. MATLAB and evidence checklist

Run the main script MIE402_Lab4_Analyze_Free_Response.m. Never run internal helper functions separately. Read usage instructions. The current main script prompts for one CSV, hardware parameters, interval and position units, and saves figures and a summary beside the CSV. Its numerical results still require evaluation against actual measurements.

For the combined Labs 4–5 report, retain the original CSV and metadata for all seven configurations, labeled plots, fitted model and residual plot, calculated physical parameters and units, frequency differences, uncertainties and a short explanation of any mismatch. Cautions include encoder slip, spring slack, dry friction, irregular sample spacing, poor peak selection and variation in added hardware mass.
