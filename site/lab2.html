Measurement path

  1. Record a full pendulum motion with the high-speed camera.
  2. Calibrate the image using the known distance between the two black marks on the arm.
  3. Track pivot and bob coordinates with Pendulum Motion Analyzer.
  4. For the standard image coordinates used by the app, convert coordinates with theta = atan2(xb - xp, yb - yp). Verify the sign convention with a frame in which the pendulum hangs vertically: theta should be approximately zero.
  5. Compare the measured period and spectral frequency with both the ideal point-mass model and the compound-pendulum model that includes the bar mass.

Air resistance and pivot friction

The compound-pendulum equation used for the main prediction includes the bob and bar masses and the restoring torque from gravity. It leaves out energy loss. In a real recording, the swing amplitude usually decreases because the moving bob and bar experience air resistance and the pivot has friction. Look for that decrease in the peaks of the measured angle trace.

An optional model adds a resisting torque to the equation of motion:

Iθ¨+Ksin⁡θ+bθ˙+q|θ˙|θ˙=0

Here I = Mℓ² + mℓ²/3 is the moment of inertia and K = (M + m/2)gℓ is the gravity-torque coefficient. The term with b is a simple linear-damping approximation. The term with q represents drag that grows roughly with the square of angular speed. Both terms oppose motion when their coefficients are positive. Pivot friction can also have a different speed dependence, so this equation is a useful extension rather than a complete description of the apparatus.

For the required Lab 2 analysis: use the undamped compound model as the initial prediction, compare the measured periods and frequencies, and describe any amplitude decay. If the data show strong decay or you want to fit a damping coefficient, document the chosen model and fit method. A single video does not by itself separate air drag from pivot friction. The longer period at large release angle also occurs in the undamped nonlinear model, so do not attribute that effect to drag alone.

Further reading: analysis of pendulum motion with linear and quadratic drag.

Key measurement checks

  • Measure bob mass M, bar mass m, and pivot-to-bob-center length l. These quantities enter the compound-pendulum prediction.
  • Record the actual camera frame rate, calibration distance, initial angle, and file name.
  • Keep the bob inside the camera frame and use a sharp, high-contrast image.
  • Use Quick Review before analyzing every frame.
  • Save raw videos and MAT files before leaving the laboratory.
