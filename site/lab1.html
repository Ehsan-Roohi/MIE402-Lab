Data Logger screen

  1. Rate: enter the required sample rate and check whether the unit is Sa/s, kSa/s, or MSa/s.
  2. Mode: use the GTA-specified mode and record it.
  3. Input: enable the microphone channel and disable unused channels.
  4. Start: choose Immediate or Delayed depending on how the sound will be produced.
  5. Duration: record at least one second for every required case.
  6. Filename: include source, reference frequency, sample rate, and trial.
  7. Record: press only when the source and settings are ready.
  8. Status: confirm that logging completed, then inspect the waveform before accepting the trial.

Export to MATLAB

Select the correct recording, choose MATLAB under Convert to, choose a destination folder, and press Download. Keep the native recording until the MAT file opens correctly.

What the MATLAB script does

  1. Loads one Moku:Go MAT file and checks its structure.
  2. Calculates the achieved sample rate from the time column.
  3. Reports sample count, duration, mean, RMS, FFT-bin spacing, and strongest non-DC peak.
  4. Creates a labeled time-domain waveform and one-sided amplitude spectrum.
  5. Replays a normalized listening copy using the measured rate or a supported resampled rate.

The listening copy is qualitative. Resampling for a sound card cannot restore frequencies already lost or aliased during acquisition.

Report focus

Compare the five rates for all three sounds in time, frequency, and playback. Predict aliasing before interpreting the FFT. Use the high-rate tuning-fork record as a reference, distinguish volts from hertz, and explain why a plausible RMS value does not prove that the frequency content was recorded correctly.
